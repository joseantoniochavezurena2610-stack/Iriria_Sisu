# El Niño in Costa Rica: 2021-2026

> **Note (18 Oct 2026):** final pilot region = Guanacaste; 100 % web app; the model only uses data from 2021 onwards. Earlier data (e.g. El Niño 2014-16) is historical context. See [`decisions.md`](../decisions.md).

Sources from the last 5 years, reviewed on 18 Oct 2026. Every fact carries its source; links are at the end (most sources are Costa Rican media in Spanish).

## 1. ENSO timeline (NOAA's official index: RONI)

NOAA now uses the **RONI** (Relative Oceanic Niño Index) as its official index. El Niño ≥ +0.5 °C; La Niña ≤ −0.5 °C.

| Period | Phase | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 – early 2023 | **La Niña** (triple dip) | Down to −1.3 °C (2022) |
| Jun 2023 – Mar 2024 | **El Niño** | Peak **+1.5 °C** (Oct–Dec 2023): moderate-strong |
| Apr – Jul 2024 | Neutral | |
| Jul 2024 – early 2026 | Weak **La Niña** | Down to −1.1 °C |
| Mar – May 2026 | Neutral | −0.4 → −0.1 °C |
| **Jun 2026 – today** | **El Niño** | Niño-3.4 at **+2.1 °C** (8 Oct 2026) and rising |

**NOAA forecast (8 Oct 2026):** **> 83 %** probability of a strong to very strong El Niño, lasting until **Jan–Mar 2027**. There is an **83 %** probability that Oct–Dec 2026 surpasses any El Niño since 1950 (RONI ≥ +2.5 °C). In parts of the eastern equatorial Pacific the sea anomaly already exceeds +4 °C.

→ **It could be the strongest El Niño on record.** For reference, 2015 reached +2.8 °C in Niño-3.4 (IMN).

> Our data pipeline reads the RONI series directly from NOAA. Because the official "5 consecutive seasons" rule cannot confirm an episode that is still under way, the 2026 El Niño is labelled from June as *ongoing* (see [`../data-sources.md`](../data-sources.md)).

## 2. El Niño 2023-24: what happened in Costa Rica

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| National temperature 2023 | **~+1 °C** above normal | IMN via Delfino, 22 Dec 2023 |
| Rainfall Jan–Nov 2023 | North Pacific **−9 %**, Central Pacific −12 %, Eastern Northern Zone −16 %, Guatuso-Los Chiles-Upala −13 %, Central Valley −2 % | IMN via El Observador |
| Full-year 2023 rainfall, Caribbean | North Caribbean **−43 %**, South Caribbean −26 % | Same |
| Cantons with their driest year on record | At least 10 (mostly Northern Zone and Caribbean) | IMN via Delfino |
| Water | AyA (national water utility): **38 %** of its systems in deficit (66 % projected for the dry season) | Same |
| Hydropower | Basins with up to **50 %** less flow | ICE via Delfino |
| Energy | **Power rationing** announced in May 2024 because of drought and low reservoirs; ICE said rain and wind reached never-recorded levels | Bloomberg Línea, 8 May 2024 |
| Some areas vs. 2015 | Quepos received half the rain of 2015 (175 vs. 350 mm) | IMN via Semanario Universidad, 19 Dec 2023 |

### ⚠️ Important finding for the engine
**El Niño 2023 barely dried Guanacaste:** the North Pacific closed at only −9 %, while the Caribbean had −43 %. Usually it is the other way round.
- **Not every El Niño hits the same way or in the same regions.** NOAA and the IMN say so as well.
- That is why the engine does not assume a "fixed −30 %". It uses the real El Niño months in the data and shows a range: 2023 (moderate, Pacific almost normal) and **2026 (the current one, already measured)**.
- This is a strong scientific argument: "the tool does not bet on a single scenario".

## 3. El Niño 2026: what is happening now

### Observed rainfall (IMN)
| Period | Fact |
|---|---|
| Jan–Jul 2026 | North Pacific **−28 %**, Central Pacific −25 %, South Pacific and Central Valley ~−21 %. North Caribbean **+21 %**, Eastern Northern Zone +19 % |
| July 2026 | Deficits of up to **−58 %** in the Pacific and Central Valley; surpluses of up to **+70 %** in the Caribbean and Eastern Northern Zone |
| Jan–Aug 2026 | North Pacific up to **−37 %** below the historical average |
| September 2026 | Deficits of up to −37 % in the Pacific and Central Valley; surpluses of up to **+42 %** in the Caribbean and much of the Northern Zone. The IMN declared a **meteorological drought** on the Pacific slope, Central Valley and Western Northern Zone |
| Canícula | Began in **late June** (earlier than normal) and lasted all of August, more intense and longer |

### IMN forecasts (how they evolved)
| Date | Forecast |
|---|---|
| Jun 2026 (for Jun–Aug) | North Pacific down to −50 %; Central Pacific and Central Valley ~−40 %; South Pacific ~−35 %; Caribbean and Northern Zone +10 to +15 %. Guanacaste up to +2 °C |
| Jul 2026 | July: North Pacific down to −70 %. Aug–Oct: North Pacific ~−60 %, rest of the Pacific and Central Valley ~−40 %. The effect could last until April 2027 |
| Oct 2026 | Effects **at least until July 2027** |

### Institutional response
| Date | Measure |
|---|---|
| 3 Jun 2026 | **Green alert** (49 cantons); **₡6,000 million** through the Guarantee Fund for preventive and emergency credit |
| 31 Jul 2026 | 17 cantons on green (some later move to yellow) |
| 19 Sep 2026 | **Orange alert in 23 cantons**: all 11 in the North Pacific (La Cruz, Liberia, Bagaces, Cañas, Tilarán, Abangares, Carrillo, Hojancha, Nandayure, Santa Cruz, Nicoya), 9 in the Central Pacific and 3 in Los Santos. **Yellow in 51 cantons. Total: 74 cantons** on alert |

### Impact on agriculture (2026)
| Impact | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rice | Rice growers in Nandayure and Jicaral **did not plant**; there will be no second planting on the Nicoya peninsula. One grower went from 350 ha (6 years ago) to 150 ha (2025) and **0 ha** (2026) | La Nación, 18 Jul 2026 |
| Maize | Production "decimated" in dry areas (UPA). In Matambú (Nicoya) the maize did not develop | La Nación; Teletica, 23 Jul 2026 |
| Tomato | **+174 %** per kilo (₡465 → ₡1,275), Jan–Jul 2026 | CNP via La Nación |
| Sweet pepper | **+49 %** per unit | CNP via La Nación |
| Onion | **+60.6 %** year on year to June | INEC via La Nación |
| Pests | Whitefly, aphids, thrips and mites, favoured by heat and drought | Caproexmes via La Nación |
| Cattle | Cutting water by up to 50 % can lower dry-feed intake by **23 %** (Corfoga); families sell animals for lack of pasture | Infobae, 25 May 2026; Teletica |
| Water | The Puerto Potrero ASADA well dropped from ~15 to 1 L/s (**> 90 %**) | Teletica, 23 Jul 2026 |

### Regional context (Central America)
- Parts of the Pacific side of Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras and Nicaragua received only **30-60 %** of average rainfall between 1 Apr and 20 Aug 2026, among the driest periods since 1981 (GEOGLAM).
- Honduras lost ~**8,772 ha of maize** and **3,080 ha of beans** in the 2026 Primera (Dry Corridor).
- This strengthens the **scale** argument: the same problem affects the whole Central American Pacific.

## 5. What this means for Iriria Sìsù
1. **Updated facts for the video:** use the **−37 %**, the **alerts in 74 cantons** and the **+174 % tomato price** (with sources). They replace the "+300 %" from 2014-15, which had no source.
2. **Engine scenarios:** normal vs **El Niño** built from the real El Niño months of **2023** (an "atypical" El Niño) and **2026** (ongoing). Show the range, not a single number.
3. **Live validation:** 2026 is a **natural experiment**. We can compare what the engine would have recommended in May 2026 with what happened (growers who did not plant, maize lost). It is a backtest with fresh data.
4. **The canícula is the critical point:** in 2026 it started earlier and lasted longer. The engine treats Primera, which contains it, as the riskiest season.
5. **The Caribbean gets wet while the Pacific dries:** the tool must be regional and must not assume the whole country moves the same way.
6. **What comes next:** the peak is Nov 2026 – Jan 2027 and its effects reach mid-2027. **The 2027 Dry season and Primera are exactly what farmers are planning now.** That is the moment the tool is useful — and the "Current forecast" scenario.
