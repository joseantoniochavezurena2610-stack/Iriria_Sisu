# Solution — v3 (18 Oct 2026)

## The problem in one sentence
In Guanacaste, the 2026-27 El Niño has already cut more than a third of the rainfall and will last at least until July 2027; grain growers decide what to plant without a tool that brings together the coming climate, their soil and their pocket.

## The idea in one sentence
**Iriria Sìsù** is a website that, using what the farmer knows about their farm and NASA's real rainfall since 2021, compares 3 three-year crop-rotation plans and shows what happens to the soil, the water, the risk and the income in a normal year and in an El Niño year.

## What changed from v2
| v2 | v3 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Streamlit (Python) | **100 % static web** (HTML/JS), engine in the browser | Opens on any phone from a link; $0; free GitHub Pages address (D18) |
| Region to be decided | **Guanacaste**, 11 cantons | Area hit hardest by El Niño |
| 30 years of climate (1995-2025) | **2021 → today** (at least 5 years) | Team rule; includes El Niño 2023 and 2026 and La Niña 2021-23 and 2024-26 |
| ESP32 sensor at the core | **The farmer enters their data**; sensor optional | No dependency on hardware |
| Live pitch + video | **Only video (≤ 30 s) + prototype** | Defined deliverables |
| 4 seasons | 3 seasons (Dry, Primera, Postrera); the *canícula* sits inside Primera and enters the risk through real rainfall | Simpler for the farmer, just as faithful |
| Parquet | Small versioned JSON generated by GitHub Actions | The website reads it directly |
| Spanish | **English** (team guides in Spanish) | Greater impact |

## Flow
1. **Data** (automatic, weekly): daily NASA POWER per canton → monthly + ET0 (Hargreaves); NOAA's ENSO index. Optional: SoilGrids (reference soil), IMERG (rainfall cross-check).
2. **The farmer** marks their canton (list, map or location), their crop, whether they have irrigation, texture (ribbon test), pH if known, slope and priorities.
3. **Engine** (in the browser): evaluates each crop in each season, rules out what is not viable, builds rotations and scores Soil / Water / Safety / Income. Details: [`engine-rules.md`](engine-rules.md).
4. **Result**: 3 plans + "if you keep planting what you plant today", with a calendar, scores, normal vs. El Niño comparison, a robust-plan label and reasons with numbers. Can be printed or shared as a link.

## Pilot crops
Rainfed rice, maize, sorghum, common bean, cowpea, peanut, melon, watermelon, mucuna and jack bean (green manures), fallow. Catalog: [`data/catalog/crops_guanacaste.csv`](../data/catalog/crops_guanacaste.csv).

**Inside the engine (since 25 Oct, D20):** income is in colones per hectare: yield × price − production cost, plus the urea a legume saves the next crop, with the maximum-tolerable-loss filter in colones. Sources and confidence of every value: [`research/prices-and-costs.md`](research/prices-and-costs.md).

## Path to impact
After the event (nothing here is needed to compete, D22): MAG/INTA extension agencies and Guanacaste cooperatives, where the extension agent opens the link during the visit, fills in the form with the grower and prints the plan. The same engine works for other regions of the Central American Dry Corridor by changing the catalog and the points.

## Market and scale (for the judges' "Impact" criterion)
- **Who uses it:** grain and horticulture smallholders on rainfed land. First Guanacaste (11 cantons), then the rest of the Central American Dry Corridor: the engine is the same, only the crop catalog, the crop-by-canton list and the points change.
- **Who pays (after the event):** nothing for the farmer. Possible adopters and payers: MAG/INTA extension services and cooperatives (planning visits), agricultural insurers and banks that lend to farmers (they need a farm-level view of climate risk), and development programmes working on El Niño adaptation.
- **Why it can scale cheaply:** static website, $0 hosting, data refreshed weekly by GitHub Actions, transparent rules instead of a black box, every number with a source.
- **Proof so far:** the 2026 backtest ([`research/backtest-2026.md`](research/backtest-2026.md)) — with data only up to April 2026, none of the plans put rainfed rice in the Primera of the 4 cantons where it later ran short of water. Caveat: at published prices rice also loses money, so part of that is a price signal, not only a water signal.

## What we do NOT promise
- We do not predict the harvest; we compare plans.
- We took no measurements on any farm: the challenge is theoretical (D23). The inputs are NASA/NOAA/FAO/ISRIC data and what the farmer enters.
- The data is regional (cells tens of km wide).
- Crop ranges come from FAO; prices and costs are recent Costa Rican averages and some are labelled estimates.
