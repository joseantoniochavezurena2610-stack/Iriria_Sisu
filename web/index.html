How do I know my texture?

Ribbon test: wet a handful of soil and squeeze it between your thumb and index finger. If it does not form a ribbon, it is sandy. If it forms a short ribbon that breaks (less than 2.5 cm), it is loam. If it forms a long, shiny ribbon, it is clay.

    How it works

    1. Real climate for your canton. We download daily rainfall, temperature and soil moisture from NASA POWER since January 2021, and compute how much water the air demands (ET0, FAO's Hargreaves method).
    2. We separate the years. Using NOAA's official index we label each month as El Niño, La Niña or neutral. That lets us compare a normal year with an El Niño year using the rain that actually fell, not an invented percentage.
    3. We test every crop in every season. Dry (Jan–Apr), Primera (May–Aug, includes the mid-summer drought or canícula) and Postrera (Sep–Dec). We check whether there is enough water (FAO-56-style balance), whether the temperature suits the crop and whether your pH allows it.
    4. We build thousands of 3-year rotations and discard those that make no sense (pH out of range, crop out of season, same family repeated).
    5. We score from 0 to 100 on four things: Soil (nitrogen, ground cover, changing families), Water (how much irrigation or deficit remains), Risk (in how many observed years water fell short) and Income.
    6. You decide the weight of each one. We show the 3 best plans and how each would do in a normal year and in an El Niño year. If it holds up in both, we label it a robust plan.

    What we do not promise

    • We do not predict your exact harvest: we compare plans against each other.
    • Satellite and model data are regional (grid cells tens of km wide). What you know about your farm (pH, texture, irrigation) is what tailors the result to your plot.
    • Crop ranges come from FAO and the literature; the team is checking them against Costa Rican sources.
    • Income is still relative (1 to 5) until prices and costs are verified.
